What actually gets made

Property advertising fails in the same way everywhere: a slow drone push-in, a piano track, a price card at the end. It looks like an advert, so it gets skipped. These are the formats that hold attention instead.

The walkthrough, with a point of view15–25s

Handheld, one continuous move through the unit, narrated like you are showing a friend — not a listing description read aloud. The camera moves at the pace a person actually walks.

“This is the cheapest 2-bed in the tower right now. Here is the catch, and here is why I would still buy it.”
The price reveal10–20s

Show the property first, price last. It is the single highest-retention format in property content because the viewer stays for the answer.

“Guess the price. Sea view, 1,450 square feet, handover next year.”
The agent, to camera20–40s

Your agent, on the balcony, saying something genuinely useful about the area or the payment plan. Builds the face people call. Also the cheapest asset you own, because it needs no location fee.

“Three things nobody tells you about service charges in this building.”
The honest area breakdown30–60s

What is good, what is not, who it suits. Saying a downside out loud is the fastest trust-builder in property content, and it filters out the wrong buyers before they waste your agent’s afternoon.

“Do not buy here if you need a school run. Here is where I would look instead.”
Statics and carouselsFeed

Floor plan, payment plan, price per square foot, handover date. Unglamorous and consistently among the best performers, because serious buyers screenshot them.

“60/40 payment plan. AED 1.9M. Handover Q3.”
The closed-deal proof15–30s

Handover day, keys, the buyer’s reaction — filmed with permission. The only content that shows you actually sell rather than just list.

“Forty-one days from first message to keys.”
Why volume matters more than polish. Creative fatigue is real: the same ad shown repeatedly to the same audience gets more expensive every week as frequency climbs. That is why the plan is a monthly shoot rather than one hero film — a steady supply of fresh cuts is what keeps cost per lead flat instead of creeping up. How we run ads

Four platforms, four different jobs.

Running the same ad on four platforms is how budgets die. Each one has a distinct role in a property sale: one captures people already searching, one creates demand at scale, two make you familiar cheaply. Here is exactly what gets built on each.

Google AdsCatch existing demand
The job
Someone is typing “2 bedroom apartment for sale in Dubai Marina” right now. They are the closest thing to a buyer that exists online. Google is expensive per click and worth every fil because the intent is already there.
Structure
  • Brand campaign — cheap, defends your own name from competitors bidding on it
  • High-intent search — exact and phrase match, one ad group per intent: buy / area / developer / project name
  • Competitor & portal terms — only once the first two are profitable
  • Performance Max — for remarketing reach across YouTube, Display, Gmail and Discover
Negatives
The single biggest saver in property search. Blocked from day one: jobs, careers, salary, rent, rental, free, map, PDF, course, agent jobs, RERA exam — and a growing list reviewed weekly from the actual search-terms report.
Landing
Never the homepage. One page per area or project, with the price, the floor plan, the payment plan and a WhatsApp button above the fold. Call extensions on, so half the traffic never needs a form at all.
Bidding
Manual CPC for the first 30 conversions, then Target CPA once Smart Bidding has enough data to be trusted. Switching too early is how people burn a month.
Reality
Fewest leads, highest price per lead, best close rate of the four. This is the channel that produces deals this quarter.
Meta — Facebook + InstagramVolume engine
The job
Nobody on Instagram woke up planning to buy a flat. Meta’s job is to create the thought, capture the enquiry cheaply, and then chase everyone who half-showed interest until they decide.
Structure
  • Broad prospecting — let the algorithm find buyers; it is better at it than manual interest stacking now
  • Lookalike 1% — built from your closed buyers, not your leads. Modelling on leads teaches it to find more tyre-kickers.
  • Retargeting — 50%+ video viewers, 180-day IG/FB engagers, 30-day site visitors, form-openers who never submitted
Creative
Six to nine assets a month, shot by me: property walkthrough reels (15–25s), agent-to-camera market updates, price-and-payment-plan carousels, one client testimonial. Reels and Stories placements first — they carry the cheapest CPMs.
The form
Instant forms with qualifying questions built in: budget band, timeline, cash or mortgage, resident or overseas. This deliberately reduces lead volume and raises lead quality — you are not paying your team to call people who were never buying.
Tracking
Pixel plus Conversions API server-side, so iOS opt-outs do not blind the optimisation. Offline conversions uploaded back from your CRM when a deal closes — that is what teaches Meta to find more buyers instead of more clickers.
Reality
The most leads per dirham of the four. Also the most noise. It works when the qualification is tight and the callback is fast.
TikTokCheap attention
The job
The cheapest attention in the market right now, because the ad auction is still immature. It buys you familiarity — and it fills the retargeting pools that Meta and Google then convert.
Structure
Broad targeting, algorithm-led. Spark Ads that promote posts already performing organically — they keep the comments and the social proof, and they cost less than cold creative.
Creative
Nine to twelve native clips a month. POV walkthroughs, “this is what AED 1.4M gets you in JVC”, price reveals, honest area breakdowns, day-in-the-life with the agent. Shot on a phone, cut fast. Anything that looks like a TV ad is skipped in half a second.
Reality
Lower intent, cheaper leads, more junk. Judged on cost per qualified lead and on how much it lowers Meta’s costs — never on raw lead count.

SnapchatLocal reach, low cost
The job
Badly underused by property advertisers in the Gulf, which is exactly why it is cheap. Snapchat’s ad reach is equivalent to 44.9% of the UAE population, and roughly half of UAE adults use it.

Snap Lead Forms plus Story ads, geo-targeted tightly to specific communities and towers rather than “Dubai”. Useful for launch pushes and open-house weekends.
Creative
Vertical, 6–10 seconds, phone-shot look, caption burned in. Same shoot day as the TikTok and Reels content — one production, four platforms.
Reality
Typically the lowest cost per lead in the mix and the lowest intent. It earns its place by making the brand familiar to a genuinely local audience for very little money.

What the first 90 days look like

Nothing is scaled before it is proven.

Meta needs roughly 50 optimisation events a week per ad set to leave its learning phase and deliver predictably. Google’s Smart Bidding wants around 30 conversions in 30 days. Those two facts dictate the entire schedule below — and they are why month one is not the month to judge results.

Days 1–30 · Build & learn

Get the plumbing right

  • Ad accounts opened in your name, spending limits set, my access added
  • Pixel, Conversions API, CRM and WhatsApp routing wired and tested end to end
  • First shoot: 12–15 assets across walkthroughs, agent pieces and statics
  • Launch Google Search and Meta only — the two that pay first
  • Learning phase respected: no panic edits, no daily budget fiddling
  • Week 4: first honest read on cost per qualified lead
Days 31–60 · Cut & tighten

Kill what lost, feed what won

  • Losing creative and ad groups switched off, not “optimised”
  • Search-terms report mined; negative keyword list expanded
  • Retargeting audiences now large enough to run properly
  • TikTok and Snapchat introduced — seeded with organic winners
  • Form questions tuned against real qualification data from your team
  • First closed deals traced back to the exact ad that made them
Days 61–90 · Scale

Push only the proven

  • Winners scaled roughly 20% every three days — faster resets learning
  • Lookalikes rebuilt from closed buyers, not from leads
  • Cost caps introduced once cost per lead is stable
  • Budget shifted toward whichever platform produces the cheapest deal
  • Quarterly review: full cost-per-deal picture, next quarter’s plan and budget

Plain English

Every term on this page, spelled out.

This industry hides behind acronyms, and a fair amount of that is deliberate — it is harder to question a number you cannot pronounce. Here is every abbreviation used above, what it stands for, and what it actually means for your money.

The money metrics

The numbers that decide whether this is working.

CPM
Cost Per Mille — “mille” is Latin for thousand
What you pay for 1,000 views of your ad. The wholesale price of attention on a platform. Snapchat and TikTok have low CPMs, which is why they are cheap places to be seen.
CPC
Cost Per Click
What you pay each time someone clicks. On Google you bid on clicks directly; on social it is a by-product of CPM and how compelling the creative is.
CPL
Cost Per Lead
What you pay for one enquiry — a submitted form or a WhatsApp message. The headline number most agencies report, and the one most easily gamed by chasing cheap, useless leads.
CPQL
Cost Per Qualified Lead
What you pay for one enquiry that is actually worth your agent’s time — right budget, real timeline, reachable. The number that matters, and the one I report first.
CTR
Click-Through Rate
The percentage of people who saw the ad and clicked it. A creative quality score in disguise — low CTR means the ad is being ignored, and the platform charges you more for it.
CVR
Conversion Rate
Of the people who clicked, the percentage who actually filled in the form. Mostly a verdict on your landing page or form, not your ad.
CPA
Cost Per Acquisition (or Action)
What one completed action costs. In property, generally used interchangeably with cost per lead; in the strictest sense, cost per closed deal.
tCPA
Target Cost Per Acquisition
A Google bidding setting where you name the price you will pay per conversion and the machine bids to hit it. Needs about 30 conversions in 30 days before it can be trusted.
ROAS
Return On Ad Spend
Revenue divided by ad spend. 3× means every dirham in returned three. In property it looks enormous because one commission dwarfs a month of budget — which is why cost per deal is the more grounded number.
Impressions
—
How many times your ad was shown. Not how many people saw it: one person seeing it five times is five impressions.
Reach
—
How many separate people saw it, counted once each. Always lower than impressions.
Frequency
—
Impressions ÷ reach — the average number of times one person saw your ad. Climbing frequency with falling results means the audience is tired of the creative.

Ad
Bottom level
The actual video, image or text a person sees. Several ads run inside one ad set so the platform can pick the winner.
Learning phase
—
The period where the platform is still working out who to show your ad to. Costs are high and unstable until it collects roughly 50 conversions in a week. Editing an ad set restarts it — which is why constant fiddling is expensive.
Optimisation event
Also: conversion event
The action you have told the platform to chase. Set it to lead and it finds form-fillers; set it to a qualified or closed-deal event and it goes looking for buyers.
Placement
—
Where the ad appears — Reels, Stories, feed, Search, YouTube. Reels and Stories usually carry the cheapest CPMs.
Bid strategy
Lowest cost / cost cap / bid cap / manual CPC
How aggressively the platform buys for you. Lowest cost spends the budget for the cheapest results it can find; a cost cap tells it never to exceed a price per result.
PMax
Performance Max
A Google campaign type that runs across Search, YouTube, Display, Gmail, Maps and Discover at once, letting Google decide the mix. Powerful, and a poor first campaign because it hides where your money went.
Advantage+
Meta’s automated campaign suite
Meta’s version of the same idea: hand over targeting and placement decisions to the algorithm. Often genuinely better than manual now, given enough conversion data.
Spark Ads
TikTok format
Putting budget behind a real organic post rather than a fresh ad. It keeps the likes and comments, so it carries social proof and usually costs less.
Instant Form
Also: Lead Form, Lead Ad
A form that opens inside Facebook, Instagram or Snapchat and pre-fills the person’s details. Very high conversion, lower intent — which is why the qualifying questions matter.
Negative keywords
Google
Words you refuse to show up for. In property this is the single biggest saving: blocking “jobs”, “salary”, “rent” and “free” stops you paying AED 40 a click for someone looking for work.
Match types
Exact / phrase / broad
How loosely Google matches searches to your keyword. Exact is tight and predictable, broad is wide and expensive if left unattended.
Search terms report
Google
The list of what people actually typed to trigger your ad — as opposed to what you thought they would type. Reviewed weekly; it is where negative keywords come from.
Quality Score
Google, 1–10
Google’s rating of how relevant your keyword, ad and landing page are to each other. Higher score, lower cost per click for the same position.

Audiences and tracking

How the platforms know who to chase — and how they learn from your deals.

Pixel
Meta Pixel / Google Tag
A snippet of code on your website that reports what visitors did — viewed a listing, started a form, submitted. Without it the platform is optimising blind.
CAPI
Conversions API
The same reporting sent server to server instead of from the browser. It matters because iOS privacy settings block a large share of browser tracking; CAPI is how you stay measurable.
Offline conversions
—
Uploading your closed deals back to the platform. This is the step that turns the algorithm from a form-filler-finder into a buyer-finder, and almost nobody does it.
Custom audience
—
A list of specific people — past enquirers, your CRM, video viewers, website visitors — that you can target or exclude.
Lookalike
LAL, e.g. “1% lookalike”
The platform finds people who resemble a list you gave it. 1% means the closest-matching 1% of the country. Build it from closed buyers, never from raw leads, or you get more tyre-kickers.
Retargeting
Also: remarketing
Advertising to people who already interacted — watched half a video, opened a form, visited a listing. Always the cheapest leads in the account, because they already know who you are.
Broad targeting
—
Giving the platform almost no audience restriction and letting the algorithm find buyers. Counter-intuitive, and now generally better than hand-picked interests.
Attribution window
e.g. 7-day click, 1-day view
How long after seeing or clicking an ad a lead still counts as caused by it. Different windows are why two platforms will both claim the same lead.
Speed to lead
—
Minutes between the enquiry arriving and a human contacting them. Under five minutes converts five to ten times better than thirty. The cheapest performance improvement available to any brokerage.
Creative fatigue
—
When an audience has seen an ad too often and stops responding. Shows up as rising frequency and rising cost per lead on unchanged targeting. The cure is new creative, not a bigger budget.
TOF / MOF / BOF
Top, Middle and Bottom Of Funnel
How close someone is to buying. Top does not know you exist (TikTok, Snapchat), bottom is ready to transact (Google Search, retargeting). Different platforms, different jobs, different expectations.
UGC
User-Generated Content
Content shot to look like a real person filmed it rather than a brand. Consistently outperforms polished production in feeds, because it does not look like an advert.

Property and local terms

Used above, and worth being precise about.

DLD
Dubai Land Department
The government body that registers every property transaction in Dubai. The source of the transaction volumes and averages quoted on this page.
RERA
Real Estate Regulatory Agency
The regulator that sits under the DLD and licenses brokers and agencies in Dubai.
Off-plan
—
Property sold before it is built. The developer typically pays the broker 4–8%, and the buyer pays no commission.
Secondary market
Also: resale, ready property
Existing, completed property sold on. The standard Dubai broker commission here is 2%, paid by the buyer.
GCV
Gross Commission Value
The full commission on a deal before any split with the agent, the brokerage or a co-broke. Property value × commission rate.
Qualified lead
—
Not a definition the industry agrees on — which is why we agree ours in writing on day one. Mine defaults to: reachable, real budget, real timeline, right market. If we do not define it, cost per qualified lead means nothing.
